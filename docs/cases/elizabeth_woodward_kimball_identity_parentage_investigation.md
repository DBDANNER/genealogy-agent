# Elizabeth Woodward K8H6-SKZ — Identity and Parentage Investigation

## Executive conclusion

Two Work phases plus ordinary-Chat peer review substantially stabilize the adult identity of Elizabeth Woodward while leaving her natal parentage unresolved.

The woman represented by FamilySearch PID **K8H6-SKZ** is established at very high confidence as the **Elizabeth Woodward who married Eliphalet Kimball at Plainfield, Windham County, Connecticut, on 10 May 1759**. Plainfield records also identify Eliphalet and Elizabeth as parents of Tammerson, born 8 March 1760, and John Lovel, born 21 January 1762. These records form a coherent adult-family cluster and strongly support **Woodward** as the surname under which Elizabeth married.

The later family trail to Cornish, New Hampshire, is strongly supported. Eliphalet appears there as a household head in the 1790 and 1800 federal censuses; later town-history and cemetery evidence place the couple in the same Cornish family cluster through 1805. Elizabeth's reported death on **4 November 1805** is strongly supported but remains derivative rather than contemporary proof.

The inherited assertion that Elizabeth was born about 1730 in **New Hampshire** is **unsupported**. It should not be used as an analytical anchor. The strongest natal candidate is Elizabeth, daughter of **Jonathan and Sarah Woodward of Plainfield**, born there 13 August 1731. That candidate fits chronology, surname, locality, and—after Phase 2—has a modest independent FAN association: a 1769 Plainfield probate commissioners' report places Eliphalet Kimball in the same estate matter as Jonathan Woodard and Jonathan Woodard Jr. The record contains no kinship language and therefore does not establish parentage.

A second woman remains a serious identity control: Elizabeth, daughter of **John and Mary Woodward of Windham**, born 16 September 1728. Phase 2 did not find a contradictory later life for her and did not eliminate her as the bride.

Accordingly, **Jonathan and Sarah remain the leading hypothesis only; Elizabeth's father and mother remain unknown**. The reasonable online road is exhausted for the project's present scope because the remaining high-value original Plainfield register and deed groups are specifically identified but access-restricted, while the remotely accessible probate and same-name routes have been pursued.

## Ordinary-Chat peer-review judgment

Phase 1 correctly stabilized the adult woman, surname, family, and Cornish trail, but ordinary Chat rejected its first claim of online exhaustion because the report itself identified several finite, potentially decisive avenues that had not yet been completed: the underlying Plainfield register, Windham/Plainfield deed records, fuller probate/distribution review, and resolution of the 1728 Windham Elizabeth.

Phase 2 was therefore limited to those closure tasks. It established that the underlying Plainfield register and the relevant Plainfield deed indexes/books are not remotely accessible under the current FamilySearch restrictions; it searched remotely accessible Plainfield and Windham probate volumes; it pursued the 1728 Windham Elizabeth; and it produced one new FAN association without producing a parental bridge.

That is sufficient to accept **online-exhausted unresolved** for this hobby project's online-only stopping rule.

One Phase 2 grading was narrowed in peer review: the inherited New Hampshire birthplace is **unsupported**, not contradicted. The surviving candidate women were born in Connecticut, but neither has been proved identical to the bride. Candidate geography cannot logically prove the opposite birthplace for an otherwise unidentified natal woman.

## Adult identity and marriage

The best inspected marriage manifestation is FamilySearch DGS **008272237**, image **8684**, a photographed Barbour-style transcription card citing **Plainfield Vital Records, vol. 2, p. 47**. It records:

> KIMBALL, Eliphalet, m. Elizabeth Woodward, May 10, 1759.

This is a later transcription of the town record rather than an image of the original 1759 register, but it preserves the exact town volume/page reference. Duplicate FamilySearch index/persona entries represent the same event and are not independent sources.

The marriage, the distinctive groom name, and the Plainfield child records make conflation with another Eliphalet Kimball–Elizabeth Woodward couple unlikely.

**Grade:** adult identity — **Established / very high confidence**.

**Grade:** Woodward as premarital surname — **Strongly supported**.

## Husband and children

Plainfield entries identify:

- **Tammerson/Tamersun Kimball**, born 8 March 1760, child of Eliphalet and Elizabeth;
- **John Lovel Kimball**, born 21 January 1762, child of Eliphalet and Elizabeth.

Later Cornish and compiled sources support additional children including Eliphalet Jr., Roxy, Samuel, and Sally, but those later children were not independently re-proved here by original birth records.

The children serve primarily as adult-identity anchors. Naming patterns were not used as evidence for Elizabeth's parents.

## Cornish, New Hampshire, trail

Eliphalet Kimball appears as a household head at Cornish in:

- the **1790 census**, FamilySearch XHKY-RG9, original image ARK 3:1:33S7-9YBZ-6G, p. 123, line 1114, NARA M637;
- the **1800 census**, FamilySearch XHR3-4P5, original image ARK 3:1:33S7-9R8F-7L6, p. 808, NARA M32.

The enumerations name only the household head, so Elizabeth's presence is an inference from the stable marriage/family and her later reported death, not an individually named census fact.

William Henry Child's later *History of the Town of Cornish, New Hampshire, with Genealogical Record, 1763–1910*, p. 246, also places the Kimball family in Cornish.

**Grade:** Cornish residence — **Strongly supported**.

## Death and burial

**Grade:** death 4 November 1805 — **Strongly supported, derivative**.

## Birthplace assessment

The inherited FamilySearch assertion of birth about 1730 in **New Hampshire** has no reviewed supporting natal source.

Two serious natal candidates found in the research were born in Connecticut, but neither has been proved identical to the married Elizabeth. Therefore the correct evidentiary statement is:

**New Hampshire birthplace — Unsupported.**

For downstream analysis, do not use New Hampshire as Elizabeth's natal origin. Do not replace it with Plainfield or Windham as an established birthplace unless a future relational bridge identifies one of the candidate women.

## Candidate parents: Jonathan and Sarah Woodward of Plainfield

Plainfield records identify an Elizabeth, daughter of Jonathan and Sarah Woodward, born **13 August 1731**. She is the strongest candidate because she matches the target's surname, age range, and marriage locality.

Phase 2 added a useful but limited contemporary association. In the insolvent estate of **Lydia Tyler**, a commissioners' report accepted **4 July 1769** lists amounts due to:

- Mr Jonathan Woodard;
- Mr Eliphalet Kimball;
- Mr Jonathan Woodard Jr.

Source: *Plainfield Probate Records 1762–1790*, FamilySearch DGS **007626786**, image **431**, original p. **171**. Related administrative entries show Jonathan Woodward handling Lydia Tyler's estate in 1769–1770.

This is independent evidence that Eliphalet Kimball and the leading Woodward candidate family were part of the same local FAN network. It does **not** state that Elizabeth was Jonathan's daughter, that Eliphalet was his son-in-law, or that the parties were heirs together.

No will, distribution, deed, guardianship, church record, or other relational record found online names **Elizabeth Kimball** as daughter of Jonathan and Sarah.

**Grade:** Plainfield 1731 Elizabeth = target — **Plausible**.

**Grade:** Jonathan and Sarah as parents — **Plausible; leading hypothesis, not proved**.

## Same-name control: John and Mary Woodward's Elizabeth of Windham

Windham records identify Elizabeth, daughter of John and Mary Woodward, born **16 September 1728**.

Phase 2 specifically attempted to trace her later life. No alternate marriage, early death, probate mention, church transfer, deed, or migration record was found that securely belongs to her. A tempting 1773 Elizabeth Woodward–Joel Ward marriage was checked and belongs to a different Elizabeth Woodward born in 1749.

Thus the 1728 Windham woman remains a viable but unlinked alternative rather than an eliminated candidate.

**Grade:** 1728 Windham Elizabeth = target — **Unknown / still possible**.

**Grade:** John and Mary as target's parents — **Weak**, because no positive association or relational bridge to the Kimballs was found.

## Final synthesis

The broken-branch investigation succeeded even though it did not identify Elizabeth's parents. It transformed a vague profile—Elizabeth Woodward, supposedly born about 1730 in New Hampshire—into a well-stabilized adult woman anchored by a 1759 Plainfield marriage, documented children, and a strongly supported later Cornish family trail.

The research also demonstrated why the most attractive parental reconstruction must remain a hypothesis. Jonathan and Sarah Woodward's 1731 daughter is an excellent contextual fit, and the 1769 probate association proves that Eliphalet Kimball interacted in the same local network as Jonathan Woodward and Jonathan Jr. But the decisive relationship statement is absent, while John and Mary Woodward's 1728 daughter remains unresolved.

The durable conclusion is therefore conservative but useful: **Elizabeth Woodward's adult identity is established; her parents are unknown; the inherited New Hampshire birthplace is unsupported; Jonathan and Sarah are the leading plausible hypothesis; and the reasonable online road is exhausted until specific restricted or newly indexed records become available.**
