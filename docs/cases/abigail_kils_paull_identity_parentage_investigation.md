# Abigail Kils/Kiles Paul — Identity and Parentage Investigation

**FamilySearch PID:** LCQS-31L  
**Status:** PEER-REVIEWED — ADULT IDENTITY AND PREMARITAL SURNAME ESTABLISHED; INHERITED FATHER CONTRADICTED; TRUE PARENTS ONLINE-EXHAUSTED UNRESOLVED  
**Peer review completed:** 28 September 2026

## Executive conclusion

FamilySearch LCQS-31L is established at very high confidence as the woman recorded at Dighton, Bristol County, Massachusetts, in the 5 December 1747 marriage intention as **Abigal Kils**, prospective wife of **James Paull**.

Her premarital surname is therefore established in the contemporary spelling **Kils**. **Kiles** is a strongly supported normalized/variant spelling and may be used analytically, while preserving the original spelling in source transcription.

The adult identity is further stabilized by Dighton births naming James Paul and Abigil as parents of Edward in 1749 and James in 1750, and by later family/death evidence culminating in the established death of widow Abigail Paull on **20 April 1813**.

The inherited exact birth year 1728 is not established. Her age at death supports a birth approximately **1727–1728**. Dighton is not established as her birthplace.

The currently attached FamilySearch father **Kiles PQ4Z-HLZ** is contradicted as represented. He is a modern placeholder added without relational evidence; his only attached source concerns an unrelated later Virginia individual.

A targeted Phase 2 tested the last concrete Dighton Kils/Kiles natal-family lead: **Sarah Talbot Kiles Bartlett**, daughter of Samuel Talbot, who was already described as a Kiles widow in her father's 1738 probate and who married Richard Bartlett at Dighton in 1739. Sarah and her unidentified first Kiles husband are chronologically and geographically compatible with being Abigail's parents, but no relational source connects Abigail to them.

No defensible father or mother was established.

**Final parentage conclusion: father unknown; mother unknown.**

The reasonable accessible online road is exhausted for the present broken-branch cycle. The original Samuel Talbot probate image specifically cited in Ancestry remains a high-priority reopening item for the project's later targeted Ancestry.com evidence sweep.
