# Nathaniel Peck M4DP-DRQ — Boston Identity and Parentage Investigation

## Executive conclusion

Three Work cycles plus ordinary-Chat peer review substantially stabilized the adult nuclear family represented in part by FamilySearch profile M4DP-DRQ, but did not identify Nathaniel Peck's parents.

The decisive contemporary evidence is Suffolk guardianship docket 12599. On 13 February 1761 Mary Peck of Boston, widow, became guardian of her son Benjamin Peck, under fourteen, who is explicitly described as a son of Nathaniel Peck, late of Boston, cordwainer, deceased. This establishes the core parent-child relationships directly.

A Boston marriage of Nathaniel Peck and Mary Marion on 25 March 1742, a First Church baptism of Mary Peck in 1743 to Nathaniel/Natanael and Mary, and the 1777 will of Samuel Peck, Boston cooper, naming mother Mary Peck, widow, siblings Mary, Hannah, Nathaniel, and Benjamin, strongly reconstruct a coherent Mary-headed household. Not every current FamilySearch child profile is independently proved identical to the people named in those records.

The inherited exact birth 25 January 1720 and exact death 13 November 1759 were not supported by contemporary evidence. A separate Boston marriage of a Nathaniel Peck to Lydia Chaffin on 16 October 1740 is real, but no evidence proved whether that groom was the same man who married Mary Marion seventeen months later.

No father or mother of the Boston cordwainer could be identified. Probate, guardianship, church, same-name, and deed work found no backward relational bridge. The final deed review affirmatively identified the most promising Mary, Samuel, and Benjamin Peck deed entries as members of other Peck families.

Final classification: **ADULT NUCLEAR FAMILY SUBSTANTIALLY STABILIZED; PARENTAGE ONLINE-EXHAUSTED UNRESOLVED.**

## Strongest evidence

### Guardianship docket 12599

Suffolk Probate file-paper collection DGS 103531714, images 918–920, contains the complete packet. The signed bond names Mary Peck of Boston, widow, and Samuel Ridgway Jr. Its condition makes Mary guardian to her son Benjamin Peck and calls Benjamin a son of Nathaniel Peck, late of Boston, cordwainer, deceased.

### Mary Marion marriage and 1743 baptism

Nathaniel Peck and Mary Marion married in Boston on 25 March 1742. A First Church index records Mary Peck baptized 27 March 1743 to Natanael/Nathaniel and Mary Peck. With the guardianship bond these records strongly support the 1742 couple as the later household.

### Samuel Peck's 1777 will

The will of Samuel Peck, Boston cooper, names his mother Mary Peck, widow, sisters Mary and Hannah, brothers Nathaniel and Benjamin, wife Sarah, and daughters Mary and Sarah. It strongly supports the later sibling cluster, although the exact identity of FamilySearch Samuel KZNQ-BMY with the testator remains unproved.

## Lydia Chaffin versus Mary Marion

Both marriage events are genuine: Nathaniel Peck–Lydia Chaffin in Boston on 16 October 1740 and Nathaniel Peck–Mary Marion on 25 March 1742. No Lydia death, occupation, church, deed, probate, or other bridge established whether these were one man or two. The correct treatment is unresolved identity.

## Deed review and stopping rule

The final deed phase inspected the actual promising records rather than stopping at index entries:

- Samuel Ridgway Jr., Suffolk 91:63: unrelated purchase, no Peck/Marion link.
- Mary Peck, Suffolk 93:183–184: Boston spinster, granddaughter of Mary Jacob, a different Peck cluster.
- Samuel Peck, Suffolk 95:257–258: Boston glazier, explicitly son of William Peck, deceased glazier.
- Benjamin Peck estate, Suffolk 100:107–109: New York City owner, explicitly son of Thomas Peck and father of six daughters.

These positive exclusions close the last identified high-value online deed avenue. No inherited property, heir language, sibling conveyance, or older Peck estate tied to the target household appeared.

## Recommended tree treatment

Do not add parents to Nathaniel Peck M4DP-DRQ. Treat the guardianship bond as the central nuclear-family source. Preserve Nathaniel as deceased by 13 February 1761 rather than asserting an exact 1759 death without evidence; treat the exact 1720 birth as unsupported; preserve Mary as mother of Benjamin and strongly supported wife/widow of Nathaniel; keep the Lydia marriage identity question open; and do not automatically place Samuel KZNQ-BMY under Mary until his identity with the 1777 testator is separately established.

No FamilySearch changes were made during the investigation.

## Final synthesis

The investigation did not identify Nathaniel Peck's parents, but it replaced a loosely assembled, precision-heavy tree profile with a defensible historical core. A Boston cordwainer Nathaniel Peck, deceased by early 1761, had Mary as the mother of his son Benjamin, and a broader Mary-headed sibling household is strongly supported. The exact birth, exact death, Lydia marriage identity, and parents remain unresolved.

Unlike after the first two phases, no specific high-value online lead remains unexamined that materially threatens the stopping decision. Nathaniel Peck is therefore banked as a completed online-exhausted unresolved parentage case, with the adult nuclear family substantially stabilized.
