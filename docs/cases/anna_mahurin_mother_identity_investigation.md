# Who Was the Mother of Anna Mahurin?

## Research target

- Traditional target: **Anna Snow**, FamilySearch **LH62-G2T**
- Husband: **David Mahurin**, FamilySearch **KPQR-2DP**
- Daughter: **Anna Mahurin**, FamilySearch **KPQR-2KC**
- Research date: **9 September 2026**
- Scope: **online and digitally accessible evidence only**

## 1. Executive conclusion

The mother of Anna Mahurin, born **17 September 1759**, is established at very high confidence as a woman recorded contemporaneously as **Anna, wife of David Mahurin**. The surviving Bridgewater family register repeatedly records the children of “David & Anna Mahurin,” including the target daughter, and the FamilySearch historical-record index independently transcribes the 1759 entry with father David Mahurin and mother Anna.

The inherited identification of this woman as **Anna Snow** is **unsupported and probably erroneous**. No contemporary record located gives the first wife the surname Snow. FamilySearch profile history shows that the profile was called **Anna Mahurin** in March 2022 and was changed to **Anna Snow** on 25 November 2022 without a newly attached source establishing Snow. A 2023 uploaded image bears a user-created title containing “Anna Snow,” but the historical image itself says only “The Birth of the Children of David & Anna Mahurin.”

The most plausible error mechanism is contamination from David Mahurin's documented later wife, **Lucy/Lucia Snow**, whom he married in 1767. Original marriage and deed records independently establish Lucy/Lucia as a Snow. Earlier or independent derivative genealogies generally distinguish the first wife as Anna (surname unknown) from the later wife Lucy Snow.

The traditional first wife's approximate **1730 birth**, **Bridgewater birthplace**, and exact **23 December 1765 death** are not established. A death before David's 1767 remarriage is plausible, and a date in or near 1765 may preserve an old tradition, but no contemporary online death record, gravestone, probate record, Bible entry, or equivalent source was found to validate the precision.

No evidence identifies Anna's parents or even produces a defensible working natal-family hypothesis. The correct project treatment is therefore:

> **David Mahurin + Anna [maiden surname unknown] -> Anna Mahurin, born 17 September 1759.**

Lucy/Lucia Snow should remain a separate later wife. No Snow or Mayflower ancestry should be attached to the mother of David's 1750-1762 children.

## 3. Audit of the FamilySearch starting identities

### Anna Snow — LH62-G2T

At the time of audit, the profile asserted:

- birth about 1730 in Bridgewater;
- death 23 December 1765;
- no parents;
- seven attached child-birth sources from 1750 through 1762.

The important findings were:

- the birth fact had **0 sources**;
- the death fact had **0 sources**;
- every attached child record identified the mother only as **Anna**, never Snow;
- the asserted 1749 marriage was not supported by an attached contemporary marriage record;
- there was no note, discussion, duplicate, or source rationale establishing Snow;
- the profile's single relevant memory was a genuine Bridgewater family-register image, but the historical heading itself reads only **“The Birth of the Children of David & Anna Mahurin.”**

The change history is particularly informative:

- **11 March 2022:** profile name recorded as **Anna Mahurin**;
- **25 November 2022:** changed to **Anna Snow**, with the generic contributor explanation “Married name used for research”;
- no newly attached source was found that established Snow;
- **19 June 2023:** exact death information was added or altered without an attached supporting source.

This creates a strong provenance warning: the profile combines a genuine first wife named Anna with later unsourced identity details.

### David Mahurin — KPQR-2DP

The investigation reconstructed a multi-wife sequence:

1. **Anna [surname unknown]** — first wife, mother recorded with children 1750-1762;
2. **Lucy/Lucia Snow** — married 1 June 1767;
3. **Ruth Dunbar** — married 12 January 1776;
4. **Sarah Basset** — married 26 November 1778; FamilySearch may label her differently because of another surname history not resolved in this case.

The later wives are supported by original or near-original marriage/deed evidence. The first wife's maiden surname and marriage date are not.

### Anna Mahurin — KPQR-2KC

Her birth on **17 September 1759** is well supported. The historical-record index names father **David Mahurin** and mother **Anna**, and the family-register image agrees. No later source reviewed provides a maternal maiden surname.

## 4. What is proved about David Mahurin's wives

### First wife: Anna [maiden surname unknown]

No first-marriage entry or intention was located online. Nevertheless, the repeated Bridgewater family-register heading and entries strongly support a stable David-and-Anna household over twelve years.

The record establishes the first wife's given name and maternal role but not:

- maiden surname;
- birth date/place;
- parents;
- exact marriage date;
- exact death date/place.

### Second wife: Lucy/Lucia Snow

This identity is independently established.

An original Easton marriage entry records **Mr. David Mehurin of Easton** marrying **Mrs. Lucia Snow of Bridgewater** on **1 June 1767**. A Bridgewater intention dated **2 May 1767** similarly names David Mahurin of Easton and Lucy Snow of Bridgewater. A 1773 deed was signed by David and Lucia Mehurin.

Published Snow reconstructions identify Lucy as a daughter of Benjamin Snow and Jemima Snell. That is relevant to distinguishing the second wife from the first, not to establishing the first wife's ancestry.

### Third wife: Ruth Dunbar

Halifax records show intentions in December 1775 followed by marriage on **12 January 1776**.

### Fourth wife: Sarah Basset

Massachusetts vital records show David Mehurin and **Sarah Basset** marrying at Mansfield on **26 November 1778**. A 1780 deed is signed by David and Sarah Mehurin.

### Consequence

David indisputably had multiple wives. Reconstructing the full wife sequence was the key step that exposed how the surname Snow could have migrated backward onto the earlier wife in a shared tree.

## 5. What is proved about Anna Mahurin's mother

The Bridgewater family register records:

- an earlier Anna Mahurin, born 26 July 1758 and died 22 October 1758;
- a second Anna Mahurin, born 17 September 1759;
- both under the family heading for the children of **David and Anna Mahurin**.

The FamilySearch historical-record index independently transcribes the 1759 entry with father David Mahurin and mother Anna.

These two presentations derive from the same underlying town-record tradition, so they are not wholly independent evidence sources, but the image/index agreement verifies the transcription and establishes that the maternal given name is not merely a modern tree relationship.

**Conclusion:** the mother of the 1759 child was Anna, wife of David Mahurin. No evidence found establishes the maiden surname Snow.

## 6. Assessment of the identity “Anna Snow”

The current evidence does not support “Anna Snow” as a defensible identity for David's first wife.

Reasons:

1. No first-marriage record located names Snow.
2. No child-birth record names Snow.
3. No deed, probate, church, Bible, cemetery, or later child record located names the first wife as Snow.
4. FamilySearch change history shows that the profile was still Anna Mahurin shortly before a contributor changed it to Anna Snow in 2022.
5. David's next wife is independently and contemporaneously documented as Lucy/Lucia Snow.
6. Earlier or independent derivative genealogies commonly distinguish Anna (surname unknown) from Lucy (Snow).
7. A later uploaded image title includes Snow, but the underlying record itself does not.

The evidence does **not** logically prove that the first wife could not also have been born a Snow. It establishes instead that **no reliable Snow identity has been demonstrated**, and the existing Snow attachment is probably the product of later-wife contamination.

Recommended label:

> **Anna [maiden surname unknown] Mahurin**

or, where software conventions make bracketed surnames awkward:

> **Anna, wife of David Mahurin — maiden surname unknown**

## 9. Local/FAN context and the Snow network

The family remained in a compact Bridgewater-Easton environment during the relevant period:

- David originated in Bridgewater;
- the first household's children were registered there through 1762;
- by 1767 David was explicitly “of Easton”;
- Lucy Snow was explicitly “of Bridgewater”;
- David and Lucia participated in Easton property transactions.

Snow was already prominent in David's kinship environment. Published reconstructions identify David's mother as Mary Snell and Lucy Snow's mother as Jemima Snell, potentially creating a close cousin relationship. Other Mahurin/Snow intermarriage also appears in the broader family network.

This network explains why the surname Snow would have been especially easy for a later compiler to associate with David's earlier wife. It does not prove that the earlier wife was born a Snow.

## 10. Candidate-family analysis

Once the Snow premise failed, a systematic search of every age-eligible Anna Snow would have become methodologically backward: it would use an unsupported surname as the search premise and create a high risk of attaching an attractive colonial pedigree by coincidence.

The only Snow family reconstructed in detail was the family relevant to **Lucy/Lucia Snow**, the proven second wife:

- Benjamin Snow;
- Jemima Snell;
- daughter Lucy/Lucia Snow.

No equivalent bridge was found for the first wife Anna.

No non-Snow natal family emerged from deed, probate, church, full-text, or derivative-provenance searching.

## 12. Final parentage conclusion

**Parents unknown.**

The responsible present conclusion is not that Anna's maiden surname is proven to be something other than Snow, but that **no reliable maiden surname has been identified** and the existing Snow label is probably erroneous.

No parental pair is sufficiently supported to name even as a working/analytical hypothesis.

The authoritative tree should therefore leave Anna's parents blank.

## 13. Important hypotheses eliminated or downgraded

1. **The first wife is securely “Anna Snow.”** Not supportable; likely a modern contamination from Lucy Snow.
2. **The uploaded 2023 family-register image proves Snow.** False; Snow appears only in the uploader's title.
3. **Lucy Snow was the mother of the 1759 Anna Mahurin.** Strongly contradicted by the distinct given names and the contemporary 1750-1762 David-and-Anna family register; Lucy's documented marriage occurred in 1767.
4. **Exact 1730/1765 dates imply old documentary precision.** Not supported by the source trail.
5. **A Mayflower-associated Snow family can be attached from surname and geography.** Methodologically rejected without an identity bridge.
6. **A later recording date of a deed implies wife overlap.** Eliminated after separating deed execution, acknowledgment, and recording dates.

## 14. Migration-Mama assessment

This case is **not primarily a genuine migration-Mama case**.

The first wife appears in a local Bridgewater household context. David's major later migration toward Vermont belongs to the family chronology after the first wife was probably dead.

The best explanation is instead an **inherited wife-identity error**, potentially amplified by later migration and family-memory simplification. Descendants could retain the memorable fact “David's wife Lucy Snow” while the earlier mother survived only as “Anna.” A later shared-tree contributor could then transfer Snow backward onto the first wife.

This broadens the project's reusable method. The general problem is not merely migrated-wife linkage but **eighteenth-century wife identity resolution**, with migration, remarriage, surname contamination, and modern tree editing all acting as possible failure mechanisms.

## 15. Methodological lessons added by this case

1. **Reconstruct the complete wife sequence early.** A later spouse can explain an earlier spouse's erroneous maiden surname.
2. **Audit FamilySearch edit history as provenance evidence.** The timing of an identity change can be more informative than a large number of attached sources that do not actually contain the claimed name.
3. **Read underlying images independently of uploader titles.** User-created labels can silently inject a modern conclusion into the apparent historical source.
4. **Grade atomic propositions separately.** The maternal given name can be established even when maiden surname, birth, death, and parents remain unknown.
5. **Distinguish deed execution from acknowledgment/recording.** Otherwise later administrative dates may create false chronology conflicts.
6. **Let a failed surname premise prune the search.** Enumerating hundreds of Snow candidates would look exhaustive while being logically misdirected.
7. **Treat migration as a hypothesis, not a template.** The Sarah and Anna cases show different mechanisms can generate superficially similar maternal brick walls.
8. **Preserve researched unknowns.** “Anna [maiden surname unknown]” is a stronger genealogical conclusion than a precise but unsupported colonial pedigree.
